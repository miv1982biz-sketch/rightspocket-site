Your credit card may already cover that broken gadget

Two benefits are quietly built into many credit cards: purchase protection and extended warranty. Surveys keep finding that most cardholders don't know they have them — which means broken phones, dead laptops and failed appliances get paid for twice. Here is how the benefits work, and the part that decides everything: the deadlines.

1. The two benefits, in plain words

Purchase protection covers eligible items you bought with the card against damage or theft for a short window after purchase — often 90 to 120 days. If a new phone screen cracks in week six, this is the benefit that may pay for the repair or replacement.

Extended warranty adds time to the manufacturer's warranty — commonly up to one extra year on eligible warranties of a few years or less. If a covered appliance fails just after the manufacturer's warranty ends, this benefit may pay for the repair instead of you.

Exact windows, caps and exclusions differ by card and network. Nothing on this page overrides your own card's terms — the document that counts is called your Guide to Benefits, and step 5 shows how to find it.

2. The condition almost everyone misses

These benefits generally apply only when the item was paid for with that card — in many cases paid in full with it. Split the payment across two cards, pay cash for part of it, or use a different card than you thought, and a claim can fail before it starts. This is also why "which card did I buy it on?" is the first question every claim asks.

3. What a claim usually asks for

Every administrator's list is a little different, but claims typically ask for the same core set: the itemized receipt, the card statement showing the purchase, a copy of the manufacturer's warranty (for extended-warranty claims), photos of the damage, and sometimes a repair estimate or a statement that the item can't be repaired. None of it is exotic — it's paperwork. The claims that fail usually fail on a missing document or a missed deadline, not on the merits.

4. Deadlines are the whole game

Purchase protection has two clocks: the coverage window itself (the 90–120 days above) and a shorter window to open the claim after the damage or theft happens. Extended-warranty claims have filing windows too. Miss one and a perfectly valid claim is simply closed. If something you bought recently breaks or is stolen, treat the claim like a same-week errand, not a someday errand.

5. Check your own card today — free, five minutes

Your issuer publishes a Guide to Benefits for your exact card. Search your issuer's site for your card's name plus "guide to benefits", open the PDF, and search inside it for "purchase protection" and "extended warranty". Note three numbers for each: the coverage window, the per-claim cap, and the claim-filing deadline. That document — not a blog post, including this one — is the source of truth for your card. Claiming is always free through your issuer's benefits administrator; anyone charging you a percentage of what you recover is selling you something the official route doesn't.
