Your product was recalled — what to do next

Finding the recall is the easy part. The refund, repair or replacement only happens if you take the next steps — here they are, in order.

1. Follow the notice about using the product

Every CPSC recall notice tells you what to do with the product right now. Many say to stop using it immediately; others say to keep it away from children, unplug it, or store it until a repair kit arrives. Do what the notice says first — the remedy process can take weeks, and the safety instruction applies from today.

2. Confirm that your exact unit is covered

A recall usually covers specific model numbers, serial or lot ranges, and manufacturing dates — not every unit with a similar name. Find the label on your product: it is typically on the underside or back, inside a battery compartment, or on a tag. Compare those numbers with the ones listed in the official notice before you contact anyone. A photo of the label saves time later.

3. Read the remedy — it is always free

The notice states the remedy the company must provide: a refund, a repair, a replacement product or part, or occasionally store credit. Remedies under a CPSC recall are free of charge, and you do not need a receipt in most cases — the model and serial details are usually enough. Be wary of any third party offering to handle this for a percentage of your refund.

4. Contact the company the way the notice specifies

Use the dedicated recall page, phone number or registration form named in the notice — not the company’s general support queue, which often cannot process recalls. Keep a record of your confirmation or reference number, and take photos before you mail anything back or make any modification the company asks for (such as cutting a cord or removing a part).

5. Do not resell it, donate it, or give it away

It is illegal to sell a recalled product in the United States — including at yard sales and on resale marketplaces — and donating one just moves the hazard to someone else. If the notice gives disposal instructions, follow them. If it does not, keep the product stored safely until the remedy is complete, then dispose of it so it cannot be picked up and reused.

6. Make the next recall find you

Most recalls are never acted on — response rates for consumer products are often in the single digits — usually because owners never hear about them. Two habits fix that: register new products with the manufacturer when you buy them, and check the products you already own from time to time.
